I strongly support the motion: **there should be strict laws to regulate LLMs**.

LLMs are not ordinary software. They can generate convincing misinformation, impersonate people, assist fraud, leak sensitive data, and amplify bias at massive scale. Unlike smaller tools, they can be deployed instantly to millions of users, making harm faster, cheaper, and harder to contain. When a technology has the power to influence elections, damage reputations, enable cybercrime, and mislead vulnerable people, leaving it largely unregulated is not innovation — it is negligence.

Strict laws are necessary for three reasons.

**First, public safety.**  
LLMs can produce harmful content with extraordinary ease: scams, deepfake scripts, illegal instructions, and persuasive falsehoods. Without binding legal standards, companies have too little incentive to prevent misuse beyond their own profit motives. Laws create accountability, forcing developers to test systems, limit dangerous outputs, and respond when harms occur.

**Second, accountability.**  
Right now, when an AI causes damage, responsibility is blurred. Was it the developer, the deployer, or the user? Strong regulation would require clear duties: transparency about training data, auditability, reporting obligations, and liability when negligence leads to harm. If no one is legally responsible, victims are left without protection.

**Third, fairness and trust.**  
LLMs reflect the biases and errors in their training data. They can discriminate in hiring, education, policing, and access to services if left unchecked. Strict laws would require independent audits, bias testing, and safeguards before deployment in high-stakes settings. That protects citizens and preserves trust in the technology.

Some argue regulation will slow innovation. But the opposite is true in the long run. Clear rules do not kill progress; they channel it. We regulate medicines, aviation, and finance not to stop them, but to make them safe enough for society to use. LLMs are powerful enough to deserve the same standard.

The question is not whether LLMs are useful. They are. The question is whether a tool that can shape public opinion, automate deception, and affect lives at scale should operate without strict legal guardrails. The answer is no.

**For the sake of safety, accountability, and trust, strict laws to regulate LLMs are not optional — they are essential.**
